# Emotions, gestures and dialogue

These constructions use recorded senses and the common grammar. They remain written-checked proposals; oral naturalness and sung realization require listening. Grammar and endings do not change to fit a rhyme.

## Lexical formations

| Form | Construction | Meaning | IPA | Syllables |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| trukoemar | trukoem + ar | hesitate, intransitive | /ˈtru.kœːm.ar/ | 3 |
| thim-raelun | hand + warmth | physical hand warmth | /ˈθim.ˌræː.lun/ | 3 |
| thaem-nael | voice + memory | mental memory of a voice | /ˈθæːm.ˌnæːl/ | 2 |
| saelraev-dathak | relief + sigh | sigh of relief | /ˈsæːl.ræːv.ˌda.θak/ | 4 |

Compounds follow modifier + head; inflect the right edge only: `thim-raeluniath`, `thaem-naelien`, `saelraev-dathakor`. Do not pluralize both components. `Trukoemar` has regular endings: `mi trukoemem`, `ti ratrukoemes`, `min vitrukoemom`. Its recorded sense licenses neither a direct object nor a passive participle.

`Trukoemen` has two regular analyses: third-person plural hesitate (`sen trukoemen`) and the genitive of hesitation (`nael trukoemen`, memory of hesitation). Syntax distinguishes them; this is not a second root.

## Required distinctions

- `Roenythar` concerns a weapon failing to fire. Feeling someone's absence is a contextual paraphrase for missing them.
- `Sloekoem` denotes bitter taste; `skaehoen` denotes resentment. Do not transfer senses automatically.
- `Sael` is soul; `naevan` is heart, organ or emotional centre. `Thaemnyr` is acoustic silence; silence does not imply consent.
- `Vael` covers desire and volition. `Kelar` denotes ability, not automatically permission.
- Chosen solitude, sadness, fear, physical stillness and emotional calm remain distinct readings. Laughter and trembling do not prove a particular emotion.
- Aliases may target an expression or a stable lexical entry (`registry: lexicon`). A short search label does not override sense restrictions.
