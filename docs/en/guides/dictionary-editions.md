# Comprehensive Vaeltharyn dictionary — working edition 1.2.0

The historical edition contains **65,000 distinct lexical spellings**, 65,001 lemma/category entries and **62,083 new defined and sourced proposals**. The 2,918 entries from 1.1.0 are retained. Conjugated forms and French cross-references are not counted as additional words.

This reaches the numerical scale of a general French dictionary, but not its level of editorial review. New forms were assigned deterministically; each French definition comes from a filtered Wiktionary sense. **Mechanically checked** does not mean **individually validated by the creator**. The meanings are proposed as working material for songs; registered forms and identifiers must remain stable.

The English `data/lexicon/en/summary.csv` provides word, category, domain, meaning, valency and stable ID. `data/lexicon/en/entries.csv` adds definitions, IPA, proposed syllable segmentation, register, source, review status and retained French provenance. IPA is calculated from written rules; no pronunciation is presented as having been tested in Suno. The [translation report](../../../archives/reports/en/translation-1.2.0.md) documents the English schema and review status.

## One defined sense per new proposal

A lookup label is a search aid; the **full definition** limits the translation. For example, the French word grave does not justify merging low pitch, danger and solemnity. An English label is equally limited by its definition. No additional sense from a source page is implicitly imported. An existing Vaeltharyn word keeps its meaning even if a French or English lookup word is polysemous.

Explicit inflections and variants, proper names, entries without usable definitions, and senses marked archaic, obsolete or rare were excluded from the new selection. Colloquial, figurative and pejorative labels remain visible when the sense was retained. This is a working selection, not an official contemporary French or English word list.

## Editorial status

The earlier 2,918 entries retain their strengths and limits. New additions do not all have contextual examples, reviewed morphological families or tested collocations. Appearance in an index does not establish a natural poetic choice. Before using a word in a song, read its entry, valency and resulting imagery; record the version of the meanings actually selected.

There is no need to approve every entry individually before writing. Use the core and useful proposals, then record decisions. Random regeneration must never rename a word already registered in a song.
