Existing `kofav`, `tronodaev`, `skithin`, `lodhom`, `moesyk` and the adjectives `fregos`, `nifen`, `skentav`, `slaroem` are reused within their definitions. An accusation or a neutral description can become insulting when directed at someone; this does not make every occurrence an insult.

## Register and word formation

`garuniv` retains its existing milder expressive meaning, comparable to “damn”. The new `vrask` is a stronger vulgar noun-modifying adjective: `ith nyr vrask`, “this fucking night”. It follows the noun; it is not treated as an English adverb placed before any adjective. Its interjection use is separately registered.

`grusk` has a vulgar substance reading and an interjection reading; `gruskar` only means to defecate. `skarn` condemns behaviour; `skarnar` describes that behaviour, without gender, parentage or sexual allegations. `grusk-tharel` and `graeth-naelkaror` follow modifier–head noun composition. Only the final element inflects: `grusk-thareli`, `graeth-naelkarori`; genitives `grusk-tharelen`, `graeth-naelkaroren`. Interjections use the existing invariant `part` category. Adjectives are not silently nominalised: `thalen fregos` retains its head noun.

Indicative force: **0** neutral, **1** mild frustration or a firm boundary, **2** informal/rude, **3** vulgar or openly insulting, **4** strongly vulgar. This is not an acoustic measurement or a universal cultural rating. Context and delivery matter; a boundary statement is not inherently an insult.

## Sound and delivery

The four new simple roots `sketh`, `grusk`, `vrask`, `skarn` use permitted onsets and syllable shapes. Their crisp onsets and short endings are a written performance proposal, not an audio-tested result. Stress remains on the root in **GRUSK-ar**, **SKARN-ar**; compounds retain first-element primary stress and second-element secondary stress. No decorative apostrophes or invented historical etymologies are used. IPA follows the written rules, without an audio claim.

## Scope and inflection

Existing definitions, songs, identifiers and licences are preserved. Words such as “fuck”, “shit”, “bitch”, “con”, “putain” and “bordel” do not have a single translation across all uses. Sex, anatomy and parentage are separate lexical senses, not inferred from pragmatic equivalents. No invented oral tradition, religious taboo or cultural history is offered as evidence.

Positive emotional profanity is distinguished from attacks: `vrask, mi velem raev!` insults no one. Human nouns retain no grammatical gender; gendered source aliases do not create gender endings in Vaeltharyn.

`gruskar`: **mi gruskem**, **ti gruskes**, **se gruske**, **min gruskom**, **tin gruskos**, **sen grusken**; past 1SG **mi ragruskem**, future **mi vigruskem**; imperatives **gruska / gruskai**.

`skarnar`: **mi skarnem**, **ti skarnes**, **se skarne**, **min skarnom**, **tin skarnos**, **sen skarnen**; past 1SG **mi raskarnem**, future **mi viskarnem**; imperatives **skarna / skarnai**. Neither verb receives a passive participle: both are intransitive.
