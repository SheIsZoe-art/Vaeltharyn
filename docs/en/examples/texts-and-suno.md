## Original song — Aelun e en naelath

**Title: Light in memory.** No word is presented as pseudo-Norse belonging to a historical language. Brackets contain English performance instructions intended to remain unsung.

```text
[Intro | Female | low, clear, intimate; no breath effects]
Mi thavem en naelkor
e mi-en thimath.
En kor nu dhave thaem;
mi vaerem se-or mi-en thael.

[Verse | Female | open vowels; steady pulse; precise consonants]
E en vornath vaenal,
mi lisem en rael.
En lym vele haelv;
en saeli thaeren kois.

[Chorus | Female | resonant sustained melody; gradual rise]
Sael, thaera naevis.
Aelun, vela e mi-ath.
Ae en saeli thaeriren
tra en thyrath naev.

[Bridge | Female | almost unaccompanied; no whispered delivery]
Mi naelem ti-en thaem.
Mi nu kaerem en kaeth.
Mi vaethem en kaerun;
en rael vyne e en dhemath.

[Final chorus | Female | full clear resonance; restrained choir answers]
Sael, thaera naevis.
Aelun, vela e mi-ath.
Min nu kaethom en nyr.
Min vithaerom thiris.

[Outro | Female | hold the final vowel; long instrumental resolution]
Aelun vele e en naelath.
Min thaerom kois.
```

### Literal translation

I hold the crystal / in my hand. / The stone has no voice; / I give it my song.

In the ancient forest, / I listen to the wind. / The moon is high; / the souls sing together.

Soul, sing deeply. / Light, remain in me. / May the souls sing / through the deep shadow.

I remember your voice. / I do not keep fear. / I open the door; / the wind comes into the house.

Soul, sing deeply. / Light, remain in me. / We do not fear the night. / We will always sing.

The light is in memory. / We sing together.

### Line analysis

- `thav-em` = hold-1SG; `naelkor` = lexicalised crystal, not “book”.
- `thim-ath` = hand-LOC; `mi-en` = my, genitive pronoun.
- `dhav-e` = have-3SG; `se-or` = to it; `thael` = song, a noun.
- `vorn-ath` = forest-LOC; `vaenal` = ancient, an invariable adjective.
- `thaer-en` = sing-3PL; `kois` = together.
- `thaer-a` = sing-IMP.SG; `naev-is` = deep-ADV.
- `vel-a` = be/remain-IMP.SG; `mi-ath` = I-LOC.
- `thaer-ir-en` = sing-SUBJ-3PL; `thyr-ath` = shadow-LOC.
- `vaeth-em` = open-1SG; `vyn-e` = come-3SG.
- `vi-thaer-om` = FUT-sing-1PL; `thir-is` here represents the lexical word `thiris`, always.

### Chorus reading aid

The following transcription is the original **French-speaker** aid. It is retained as a performance annotation; English readers should use the [IPA pronunciation guide](../reference/phonology.md).

`Sael, thaera naevis` → **SAÈÈL, THAÈÈ-ra NAÈÈ-vis**.

`Aelun, vela e mi-ath` → **AÈÈ-loun, VÈ-la è MI-ath**.

`Ae en saeli thaeriren` → **AÈÈ èn SAÈÈ-li THAÈÈ-ri-rèn**.

`Tra en thyrath naev` → **tra èn THÜ-rath NAÈÈV**, where Ü indicates the French u vowel /y/.

This transcription is approximate. Long vowels may span several notes; duration does not add a syllable.

## Suno style ready to test

Order: genre → voice → percussion → plucked strings → bass → other instruments → arrangement → production → ending. This test uses [Female] in each song section; the language itself does not require a female voice.

```text
Mystical Nordic-Celtic inspired ritual folk, 68 BPM, 6/8. Female lead: clear, close, resonant low-mid register opening into luminous sustained high notes; precise invented-language diction, rolled r, pure long vowels, expressive legato. Frame drums enter softly, deepen beneath the chorus, then stop for the bridge. Nylon-string guitar sustains sparse open arpeggios; no electric guitars. Warm bowed double bass anchors slow roots. Lyre answers the voice; low strings rise gradually; a restrained mixed choir supports only the final chorus. Preserve one memorable vocal melody through the section contrasts. Spacious natural hall, present lead, textured acoustic detail, controlled low end. End with a held vowel and a long instrumental resolution.
```

Starting settings, to be adapted to the controls actually available: low to moderate weirdness; high, but not maximum, style adherence. Optional exclusions: pop beat, EDM drop, trap hats, metal guitars, vocal stutter, chopped syllables, distant lead, abrupt ending. None of these instructions guarantees engine behaviour. Listen especially for digraphs, stress, word integrity and sustained endings.

## Listening protocol

1. Test the chorus alone, with the same voice and reference melody if available.
2. Compare canonical spelling with faithful ASCII without changing the style at the same time.
3. Record the spelling, the pronunciation heard and the desired correction.
4. Keep phonetic adaptations in a performance copy; do not rewrite the dictionary.
5. Then validate the whole song. A satisfying musical rendition does not validate every linguistic form improvised by the engine.
