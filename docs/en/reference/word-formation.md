# Word formation

## Principle

A root is invented lexical material; a derived form is built through a stated rule. Do not invent a new translation of an established canonical word to improve a rhyme. Poetic vocabulary retains stable meanings.

## Derivation

| Base | Operation | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| verb stem | + eth | thaereth | the action of singing |
| verb stem | + or | thaeror | a person who sings |
| verb stem | + el | thaerel | an instrument intended for singing |
| noun | + va | saelva | relating to the soul |
| adjective | + eth | naeveth | depth |
| adjective | + is | naevis | deeply |
| noun | + il | saelil | little soul; diminutive |
| noun | + aun | saelaun | great soul; augmentative |
| noun or adjective | prefix ul- | ulsaev | not beautiful, ugly |
| noun | + ar | saelar | to embody/manifest a soul |
| verb stem | + av + ar | neravar | to make someone sleep |

Forms are written together and all vowels are retained. The suffix -or has two distinct uses: the dative case on a complete noun, and an agent noun on a verb stem. `thaeror` is a singer; `thaeroror` means “to the singer”. Segmentation may be ambiguous, as in natural languages; context and the dictionary resolve it. Avoid unnecessary collisions when creating words.

The prefix ul- expresses privation or a regular lexical opposite; it never negates a clause. For a culturally specific opposite or a complex nuance, add a distinct dictionary root. Deriving a verb from a noun with -ar does not automatically make it transitive: it means “to manifest X”, intransitively unless an entry explicitly states otherwise. An already registered verb takes precedence over this derivation: do not reinterpret `thalar`, to live, from `thal`, arm.

## Compounding

Two nouns combine in **modifier + head** order: `sael-aelun`, soul-light; `naur-thael`, fire-song. The hyphen is obligatory and distinguishes a compound from a simple root. Only the final element receives number and case: `sael-aeluniath`, in the soul-lights.

Compounding does not replace every possessive construction. `aelun saelen` describes a light belonging to a soul; `sael-aelun` names a kind of light. An adjective remains before the noun inside a compound only when that lexical compound has been registered; otherwise the adjective follows the noun normally.

Possible transparent terms, not counted as independent roots:

- `sael-aelun`: soul-light.
- `naur-thael`: fire-song.
- `lym-vareth`: moon-path.
- `syr-nael`: star-memory.
- `dhor-aelun`: light of the underground depths.
- `vael-thael`: song of desire or will.

## Proper names and loans

Personal names remain recognisable: `Zoé`, `Keth`. A hyphen separates their suffixes: `Zoé-en`, of Zoé; `Keth-or`, to Keth. Do not translate a name through an etymology invented after the fact. Proper names need not follow the language's alphabet.

For modern technologies, form a descriptive compound when it is clear; otherwise register an adapted loan and identify it as such. The initial canon favours the sensory world, relationships, creation and singable images; it does not claim to contain all scientific terminology from the outset.

## Adding an entry

1. Define its exact meaning and grammatical category.
2. Search both indexes for an existing entry.
3. Prefer transparent derivation when appropriate.
4. Check spelling, sound, homonyms, plurals and conjugation.
5. Add a translatable example and update both dictionaries.
6. Record the date and version in [Canon and checks](canon-and-controls.md).
