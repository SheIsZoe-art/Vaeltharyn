# Conjugation

## One class, no irregular verbs

Every verbal infinitive ends in **-ar**. Remove exactly those final two letters to obtain the stem. Add any mood marker, followed by the personal ending. Tense is expressed by a prefix before the stem. Stress remains on the root.

**Formula: tense prefix + stem + mood suffix + person.**

| Person | Pronoun | Ending | thaerar, to sing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 singular | mi | -em | thaerem |
| 2 singular | ti | -es | thaeres |
| 3 singular | se | -e | thaere |
| 1 plural | min | -om | thaerom |
| 2 plural | tin | -os | thaeros |
| 3 plural | sen | -en | thaeren |

Singular noun subjects use -e; plural noun subjects use -en. Coordinated subjects including the speaker take -om; without the speaker but including the addressee, -os; otherwise -en. A suffix causes neither elision nor mutation.

## Tense

| Tense | Prefix | 1SG example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| present | zero | mi thaerem | I sing |
| past | ra- | mi rathaerem | I sang / was singing / have sung, depending on context |
| future | vi- | mi vithaerem | I will sing |

The past tense alone does not distinguish the French imperfect, simple past and compound past. Aspect makes such distinctions explicit when needed.

## Aspect

Aspect particles are **invariable** and stand between the subject and negation/verb. Order: subject – aspect – negation – verb – adverb – object. The initial canon allows one aspect particle per verb.

| Particle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| zero | neutral | mi rathaerem, I sang / was singing / have sung |
| ela | progressive, ongoing | mi ela rathaerem, I was singing |
| dha | completed; prior to the reference point | mi dha thaerem, I have already sung |
| ara | habitual | mi ara rathaerem, I used to sing / habitually sang |
| inu | beginning | mi inu thaerem, I begin to sing |
| alu | continuation | mi alu thaerem, I continue singing |

`mi dha rathaerem` = I had sung; `mi dha vithaerem` = I will have sung. Completed aspect with the present refers to a present result of an earlier action. `mi ela nu thaerem` = I am not singing: negation scopes over the whole progressive construction. To negate only another proposition, express it in a subordinate clause.

## Moods

| Mood | Marker | 1SG | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| indicative | zero | thaerem | assertion |
| subjunctive | -ir- | thaerirem | wish, purpose, unreal hypothesis |
| conditional | -el- | thaerelem | conditional action |

Tense markers combine with these moods: `rathaerirem`, a past subjunctive reading such as “that I may have sung” or “that I sang”, depending on context; `rathaerelem`, I would have sung. Completed aspect makes anteriority explicit: `mi dha rathaerelem`, I would already have sung at that point. The present conditional has no tense prefix; do not automatically add vi- to express “I would sing”.

An independent wish begins with `ae` and takes the subjunctive: `ae en saeli thaeriren`, may the souls sing. The particle `ae` has the same spelling as the digraph, but is an independent grammatical word.

## Full paradigm of thaerar

| Pronoun | Present | Past | Future | Present subjunctive | Present conditional |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mi | thaerem | rathaerem | vithaerem | thaerirem | thaerelem |
| ti | thaeres | rathaeres | vithaeres | thaerires | thaereles |
| se | thaere | rathaere | vithaere | thaerire | thaerele |
| min | thaerom | rathaerom | vithaerom | thaerirom | thaerelom |
| tin | thaeros | rathaeros | vithaeros | thaeriros | thaerelos |
| sen | thaeren | rathaeren | vithaeren | thaeriren | thaerelen |

The past and future of each mood are formed by adding ra-/vi- to the corresponding form. Aspect particles precede the verb form. There is no additional irregular class to learn.

## Imperative and non-finite forms

| Form | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| infinitive | stem + ar | thaerar, to sing |
| singular imperative | stem + a | thaera, sing! |
| plural imperative | stem + ai | thaerai, sing! (plural) |
| active participle | stem + in | thaerin, singing / that sings |
| passive participle | stem + un | thaerun, sung |
| action noun | stem + eth | thaereth, the act of singing |

Participles are adjectival, with no person, tense or agreement. Use a full clause to express a temporal relation when needed. `en thael thaerun`, the sung song. A passive participle requires a transitive meaning: adding a suffix does not make an intransitive verb transitive.

## Voice and valency

Active: `mi thaerem en thael`, I sing the song. Passive: patient subject + **conjugated velar + passive participle**. An optional agent is introduced by `fra` with a locative nominal: `en thael vele thaerun fra mi-ath`, the song is sung by me. This agentive use of `fra` is reserved for the passive; elsewhere `fra` expresses spatial origin. `en thael ravele thaerun`, the song was/has been sung. Progressive passive: `en thael ela vele thaerun`.

A transitive verb may omit an object recoverable from context: `mi lisem`, I listen. An intransitive verb does not take a direct object unless the dictionary permits it. Regular causative `-av-` precedes -ar: `nerar`, to sleep → `neravar`, to make someone sleep → `mi neravem se`, I make them sleep (singular). The causer is the subject; the affected person becomes the object. With a transitive base verb, the second participant is dative: `mi thaeravem se en thaelor`, I make them sing the song. The verb in -av- identifies this causative use of the dative.
