# Grammar

## Architecture

The language uses regular suffixes, with no grammatical gender, adjective agreement or irregular verbs. Neutral word order is **subject – verb – object**. Everyday speech and singing use the same grammar. Ritual style is distinguished mainly by prosody, imagery and an explicitly marked emphasis construction.

## Nouns and cases

The dictionary form of a noun is singular in the direct case. The plural adds `-i`; the case suffix follows the number suffix. No letters are removed. Adjacent vowels are pronounced in hiatus.

| Function | Suffix | sael, soul | sael-i, souls |
|---|---|---|---|
| subject or direct object | zero | sael | saeli |
| possessor, nominal source | -en | saelen | saelien |
| recipient, beneficiary | -or | saelor | saelior |
| location, domain | -ath | saelath | saeliath |
| means, instrument | -ul | saelul | saeliul |

In the direct case, word order distinguishes subject from object. A genitive follows the possessed noun: `aelun saelen`, the light of the soul. A dative follows the direct object: `mi vaerem aelun ti-or`, I give you light. The locative may stand alone to frame a clause; a preposition specifies the spatial relationship. The instrumental expresses means, without automatically implying accompaniment.

Spatial prepositions govern the locative, including for movement; direction is expressed by the preposition. `e saelath`, in the soul; `to dhemath`, towards the house; `fra dhemath`, from the house. Non-spatial `ko`, with, and `sen`, without, govern the direct case: `ko ti`, with you. `mi thaerem thaemul`, I sing with the voice, expresses an instrument.

## Determiners

No article is obligatory: `sael` can mean soul, a soul or the soul, depending on context. Optional definite `en` precedes the whole noun phrase. Explicit indefinite `un` is also the numeral one. Demonstratives `ith`, this, and `oth`, that, precede the noun. Do not combine an article with a demonstrative. Quantifiers precede the noun; a numeral greater than one requires a plural noun.

Adjectives follow the noun and are invariable: `en saeli naev`, the deep souls. Order: determiner – quantity – inflected noun – adjective – possessor. `en saeli naev mi-en`, my deep souls. The article does not take a case suffix.

## Pronouns

| Person | Direct | Genitive | Dative | Locative | Instrumental |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | mi | mi-en | mi-or | mi-ath | mi-ul |
| you, singular | ti | ti-en | ti-or | ti-ath | ti-ul |
| he, she, singular they, it | se | se-en | se-or | se-ath | se-ul |
| we | min | min-en | min-or | min-ath | min-ul |
| you, plural | tin | tin-en | tin-or | tin-ath | tin-ul |
| they | sen | sen-en | sen-or | sen-ath | sen-ul |

A pronominal possessive may also immediately precede the noun: `mi-en sael`, my soul. It remains after any determiner: `en mi-en sael`. This alternative position is reserved for possessive pronouns, not nominal genitives.

Hyphens in pronominal forms are obligatory in canonical spelling to keep their structure transparent. Ordinary nouns are written together: `saelen`. `sen` can also be the preposition “without”. Position distinguishes the uses: before a verb, it is a subject pronoun; before a noun phrase, a preposition. To avoid ambiguity, use a clause with `nu` rather than an unclear sequence.

Reflexive `si` takes the same hyphenated case suffixes: `mi maelem si`, I love myself. Use reflexive possessive `si-en` when the possessor is the subject of the clause. Invariable reciprocal `sirin` follows a transitive verb: `min maelom sirin`, we love one another.

Subject pronouns are normally expressed. They may be omitted when the endings suffice and context remains unambiguous. A plural noun takes the third-person plural verb form.

## Adjectives, comparison and adverbs

`velar` acts as the copula: `en sael vele naev`, the soul is deep. Adjectives are not conjugated.

Comparative: `mer + adjective + ta + direct-case comparison term`. `en dhem vele mer thar ta en vrel`, the house is bigger than the tower. Equality: `sam + adjective + ta`. Superlative: `maer + adjective`, the most…; specify the comparison group with `e` and the locative. Intensive: `sha + adjective`, very…

A derived adverb adds `-is` to the adjective: `naev-is` → **naevis**, deeply. The adverb follows the verb and precedes the object; a sentence adverb may be initial. `mi thaerem naevis`, I sing deeply. Lexical adverbs of time and place are invariable.

## Negation and questions

`nu` precedes the conjugated verb: `mi nu rathaerem`, I was not singing / I did not sing / I have not sung, as context requires. Tense prefixes remain attached to the verb. `nul` means no/none and precedes a noun; negative concord is not obligatory. `mi mirem nul aelun`, I see no light.

A yes/no question is a declarative clause followed by `ma`: `ti thaeres ma ?`, do you sing? Replies: `ei`, yes; `nau`, no. Question words occupy the position of the requested information: `var thaere ?`, who sings? `ti mires ven ?`, what do you see? `ti vele ver ?`, where are you? `ti vithaeres van ?`, when will you sing? `ti thaeres veth ?`, how do you sing? `ti thaeres vor ?`, why do you sing? `ti vaeres vaen saeli ?`, how many souls do you give? Nominal interrogatives take case suffixes: `varor`, to whom; `varen`, whose/of whom. Do not add `ma` when a question word is present.

## Coordination and subordination

`an`, and; `ath`, but; `or`, or. A conjunction connects units with the same role. Repeating the same case on each noun makes scope explicit: `e dhemath an vrelath`.

| Subordinator | Meaning | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| dhe | that; complement clause | verb + dhe + complete clause |
| ir | if; condition | ir + clause, clause |
| thar | because | clause + thar + clause |
| val | when | val + clause, clause |
| vora | so that | clause + vora + subjunctive clause |
| lei | although | lei + clause, clause |
| ya | relative marker | antecedent noun + ya + clause with a gap |

Subject relative: `en thalen ya thaere`, the person who sings. Object relative: `en thael ya mi thaerem`, the song that I sing. For an oblique relation, retain a resumptive pronoun: `en thalen ya mi vaerem aelun se-or`, the person to whom I give light. This pronoun refers back to the antecedent, not to another person.

Complement clause: `mi sendem dhe ti thaeres`, I know that you sing. Open condition: `ir ti thaeres, mi lisem`, if you sing, I listen. Unreal condition: subjunctive in the condition and conditional in the main clause: `ir ti thaerires, mi thaerelem`, if you sang, I would sing.

## Infinitives, arguments and modality

A conjugated modal is followed by an infinitive sharing its subject: `mi vaelem thaerar`, I want to sing. When the subjects differ, use `dhe` + a complete clause. After a noun, a purpose infinitive is introduced by `vo`: `en dhem vo thaerar`, the house for singing. `vo` is not a spatial preposition.

An infinitive may be a subject: `thaerar vele saev`, singing is beautiful. For a nominalised object, prefer the derivative in `-eth`: `mi maelem thaereth`, I love the act of singing.

## Emphasis and imperatives

`ke` introduces **verb – subject – object** order: `ke thaere en sael`, then sings the soul / the soul sings, with ritual emphasis. `ke` changes neither tense nor lexical meaning. Without `ke`, retain SVO. Do not put an object before the subject unless it is explicitly named in a detached construction: `en thael, mi thaerem se`, that song, I sing it.

Singular imperative: stem + `a`; plural: stem + `ai`. `thaera !`, sing! (singular); `thaerai !`, sing! (plural); `nu thaera !`, do not sing! Hortative `la` + first-person plural present: `la min thaerom`, let us sing. A vocative may stand at the beginning, separated by a comma: `Sael, thaera !`, soul, sing!

## Numbers and quantity

Base ten: `nul` 0, `un` 1, `dai` 2, `tryn` 3, `keth` 4, `pyr` 5, `sul` 6, `vaith` 7, `oel` 8, `neth` 9, `dek` 10. `dek-un` 11; `dai-dek` 20; `dai-dek-un` 21. `khar` 100, `myr` 1000; `dai khar tryn-dek` 230. Hyphens join elements within a tens group; spaces separate hundreds/thousands groups. Multipliers precede `khar/myr`; a multiplier of one may be omitted. Decimals: `pel` between the integer part and individually spoken digits. Fractions: numerator + `del` + denominator. `un del dai`, one half.

Ordinals add `-ith` to the final element: `daiith`, second; `dai-dek-unith`, twenty-first. Concatenate without removing letters. An ordinal precedes a singular noun: `daiith thael`, second song.

Mass quantity: a singular noun with `velu`, much, or `niri`, little. Count quantity: `velu saeli`, many souls. `vethen`, each, requires a singular; `sava`, all, requires a plural. No distinction of natural gender is required. Sex or gender information may be specified lexically when useful.
