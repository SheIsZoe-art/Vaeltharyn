## Coverage actually developed

Everyday life (body, meals, housing, clothing, relationships, calendar and movement), the sensory world (animals, plants, climate and landscapes), creation (arts, storytelling, music, diction and sound production), collective life (work, economy, school and institutions) and basic technical concepts (measurement, matter, energy and computing) now have distinct entries. Kinship words are no longer limited to parents and collective siblinghood. Distinctions between sound/music, language/anatomy, silver/money and computing/everyday senses are explicit.

## What is not exhaustive

- Lists of species, diseases, medicines, molecules, occupations and specialities are incomplete.
- Proper names, brands, real places and historical names are not arbitrarily invented or translated.
- There is not yet an extensive conversational corpus, collocation dictionary or validated cultural phraseology.
- Proposed words have not been tested in singing; illustrative constructions are written examples.
- Not every ambiguity between lemmas and inflected forms can be removed without changing the initial canon.
- Technical meanings have working definitions; they are not standardised professional terminology.
- A productive language can create new compounds: no finite inventory contains every possible expression.

The completeness achievable here concerns **indexing all registered entries** in both directions and **the regular paradigms of all registered verbs**. It does not mean every French or English text can be translated word for word. For a missing sense, use a documented paraphrase rather than inventing a translation during a song.

## Targeted future extensions

Useful next stages are semantic review by domain, translated dialogues, contextual examples by word family and specialised vocabulary based on texts actually written. Suno phonetic variants must remain separate from lemmas. Automatic additions must not present proposals as an already validated canon.
