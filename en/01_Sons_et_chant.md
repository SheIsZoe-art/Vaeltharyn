# Sounds and singing

## Canonical alphabet

Latin script: `a á ae b d dh e é f g h i í k l m n ng o ó oe p r s t th u ú v y ý`. Each digraph represents one sound. There are no silent letters, French-style nasal vowels, initial mutations or obligatory historical sound changes.

| Spelling | IPA | Pronunciation guide |
|---|---|---|
| a / á | /a aː/ | open a; short / long |
| e / é | /ɛ ɛː/ | vowel of English *bed*; short / long |
| i / í | /i iː/ | pure ee sound; short / long |
| o / ó | /ɔ ɔː/ | open o; short / long |
| u / ú | /u uː/ | pure oo sound; short / long |
| y / ý | /y yː/ | French u or German ü; short / long |
| ae | /æː/ | long vowel near the a in English *cat* |
| oe | /œː/ | long open rounded front vowel, as in French *cœur* |
| ai | /ai̯/ | a followed by i within one syllable |
| ei | /ɛi̯/ | open e followed by i within one syllable |
| au | /au̯/ | a followed by u within one syllable |
| oi | /ɔi̯/ | open o followed by i; never the French /wa/ sound |
| th / dh | /θ ð/ | th in English *thin* / *this* |
| ng | /ŋ/ | final sound of English *sing*; one sound |
| r | /r/ | rolled or tapped r; a throat r is not required |
| g | /g/ | always hard, as in English *go* |
| s | /s/ | always voiceless, including between vowels |
| h | /h/ | a lightly articulated breath |
| other consonants | /b d f k l m n p t v/ | their usual IPA values |

Different adjacent vowels not listed as digraphs are pronounced in hiatus: `ia` = /i.a/. Two identical vowels meeting at a morphological boundary remain two syllables; a dot may separate them in an annotation. A vowel is lexically long only when marked with an acute accent or written `ae/oe`; a melody may sustain it without changing the word.

## Stress and syllables

Lexical stress falls on the first syllable of the root and never shifts after suffixation. The tense prefixes `ra-`, `vi-`, `ru-` remain unstressed. A compound has primary stress on its first root and secondary stress on its second. Particles do not normally bear sentence stress.

Examples: `THAER-ar`, `ra-THAER-em`, `SAEL-i-ath`, `SAEL-aelun`. `ae` counts as one syllable nucleus, despite its length. Thus `thaerem` has two syllables: `thae-rem`.

Usual syllable structure: (C)(C)V(C)(C). Roots favour one or two syllables; derived words may be longer. Permitted initial clusters: an obstruent + l/r/v, or s + p/t/k/m/n/l/v. `th+r` is permitted. More difficult sequences across morpheme boundaries are still pronounced distinctly. A very brief **phonetic** schwa may be inserted, without writing it or counting it as a canonical metrical syllable.

## Singing

- Sustain the vowel, then close the consonant: `thaaaer-m`; do not multiply the r sounds.
- Prefer line endings in `á, ae, ó, ú, l, n, r, m`.
- In a choir, synchronise final consonants after the sustained vowel.
- Grave accents and stress symbols used in reading aids are not part of canonical spelling.
- Do not add decorative apostrophes: they encode no feature of the language.
- Sung and spoken texts follow the same grammar.

## Two working transcriptions

**Faithful ASCII:** á=aa, é=ee, í=ii, ó=oo, ú=uu, ý=yy; retain ae/oe/th/dh/ng. This is convenient for entering lyrics, but doubled vowels are not canonical spelling.

**French-speaker reading aid:** u→ou, y→u, ae→aèè, oe→eu, th→th, dh→dh, r→rolled r. These substitutions are intended for French readers. They are neither an alternative alphabet nor a guarantee that a music engine will pronounce words correctly.

In Suno, start with canonical spelling. If a word resists, test a transcription for that word alone. Keep the canonical text separate from the performance version. The realisation of /θ ð æː œː y/ may vary with the engine and voice.
